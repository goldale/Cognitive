Chapter 3

Information and Communication

Section 3.6

Higher-Level Communication Phenomena

Human debates, teaching, literary interpretation, and cultural communication include computational integration together with social and emotional objectives. The current architecture models the integration layer first.

The working hypothesis is that many higher-level phenomena will emerge when persistent agents with different objectives repeatedly serialize, integrate, act, remember consequences, and model one another.

Language diversity may increase population-level cognitive variability. A single language maximizes immediate interoperability; multiple languages can preserve different category systems, compression schemes, and optimization paths.

Reinterpretation of the Babel story

From a population-optimization perspective, language fragmentation can be interpreted not only as communication loss but also as an increase in model diversity and an additional dimension for evolutionary search.

Not an established historical claim

The Babel interpretation is an architectural analogy, not a claim about the historical origin or purpose of human language diversity.
