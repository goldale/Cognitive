Chapter 10

Associative Memory

Section 10.15

Vertex-Owned Associative Memory and Conservative Dictionary Evolution

Canonical vertex model

A vertex is an identifier of a concept in the associative dictionary and a point of long-term statistical observation of its use.

  • Each vertex owns a bounded Edge List, which constitutes the persistent associative memory of the concept.
  • Each vertex provides a local search function for locating relationships within its Edge List.
  • Each vertex owns long-term Usage Statistics accumulated from the concept’s participation in completed Logical Episodes.

Bounded Edge List

The Edge List grows while free capacity remains. During sleep, conservative offline maintenance consolidates, merges, weakens, or removes edges and thereby restores free capacity for subsequent operation. Emergency replacement at saturation may exist as an implementation safeguard, but it is not the primary memory-maintenance mechanism.

Transient semantic-search paths

The complete multi-hop semantic-search path is never stored as a persistent memory structure. Local Edge Search returns transient matching edges for the current retrieval wave. The persistent source of truth remains the bounded Edge List owned by each vertex.

Usage Statistics

Usage Statistics summarize the long-term use of the concept across completed Logical Episodes. They may characterize activation frequency, temporal distribution, contextual use, coactivation patterns, contradictory evidence, and persistent internal or cross-concept regularities. They do not preserve complete Logical Episodes or transient multi-hop paths.

Associative Dictionary Evolution

Concept Differentiation

A previously broad concept may differentiate into multiple independently meaningful concepts when long-accumulated Usage Statistics reveal stable and repeatedly confirmed distinctions. For example, an initially broad concept such as chair may differentiate into chair, armchair, and stool.

Concept Generalization

Multiple existing concepts may support creation of a new higher-level concept when long-accumulated Usage Statistics reveal a stable shared semantic structure. Differentiation and generalization are complementary processes of associative-dictionary evolution.

Conservative dictionary evolution

The associative dictionary is one of the most conservative structures of the cognitive architecture. Although creating a vertex may be inexpensive in hardware, dictionary modifications are intentionally rare and depend on statistics accumulated over many independent Logical Episodes and multiple sleep cycles. A single observation or Logical Episode must not directly create, split, or generalize a concept.

New vertex formation

A new vertex is a new entry in the associative dictionary. It may represent a previously unknown concept, a differentiated concept, or a new generalizing concept. It is never created merely as an additional storage container. Any increase in memory capacity is a secondary consequence of semantic development.

Sleep-time analysis

Sleep may analyze Usage Statistics, maintain bounded Edge Lists, and form or re-evaluate conservative candidates for concept differentiation or generalization. Dictionary changes occur only after long-term statistical confirmation; sleep does not make the associative dictionary rapidly plastic.
